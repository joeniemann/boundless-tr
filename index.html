The method

Assess. Rebuild.
Load. Release.

Visit 1

Assess

Ninety minutes, one-on-one. History, movement screen, strength and range testing. We find the actual limiter, not the loudest symptom.

Weeks 1–4

Rebuild

Restore range, calm the irritated tissue, and rebuild the base capacity the injured area lost. Hands-on work only where it earns its place.

Weeks 4–10

Load

Progressive loading against measured targets — force, tolerance, speed. This is where rehab becomes training and the gap closes.

Weeks 10–12

Release

Sport-specific testing and a written program you keep. You leave with a standard to hold yourself to, not a hope.

Honest about fit

Is this you?

A good fit

  • You were discharged from PT but still don't trust the knee, shoulder, or back.
  • You're post-op and want a plan that ends at your sport, not at a range-of-motion goal.
  • You lift, run, or compete and want to keep doing it for another twenty years.
  • You'd rather have hard numbers than reassurance.

Not a fit

  • You want passive care — heat, stim, and a table nap.
  • You need urgent or emergency medical evaluation.
  • You're looking for the fastest possible discharge note.
  • You don't want to do work between sessions.

Before you book

Questions.

Do I need a referral?

In most cases no — you can be evaluated directly. If your situation needs a physician's input, we'll tell you at the evaluation and coordinate rather than string you along.

How does payment work?

Prices are on the service menu above. The Revival Session and the Boundless Evaluation are $90 each. Packages need a Boundless Evaluation first, and we'll walk you through the options at the consult, including documentation you can submit for out-of-network reimbursement or HSA/FSA use. You'll know the exact cost of your plan before you commit to it.

How is this different from regular PT?

Two things: you work with the doctor every session, and the plan doesn't end when the pain does. Most clinics discharge at pain-free. We keep going until you've tested ready for the thing you actually want to do.

What should I bring to the first visit?

Training clothes you can move and sweat in, any imaging or surgical notes you have, and a clear idea of the activity you want to get back to. That goal shapes the whole plan.

Can you work with me remotely?

Yes — remote programming and video check-ins are available once we've done an in-person evaluation, or on their own for straightforward strength and load management work.

How young do you go?

Middle school and up. Younger athletes get a plan written around their growth stage and season calendar, and parents are welcome in the room for the evaluation.
